## 2. The rules in short

1. **The fee never appears in a cold email.** No 5%, no "pay", "money" or "commission", no referral mechanics. It belongs on the call, in the one-pager (only to people who have replied), and with signed partners.
2. Open with "Jesse from Smart Contracting here. We're a local Christchurch drainage team." Property managers get the 20 Oct opening.
3. One verified detail about them, rounded off so it connects to us. Never a bare fact. Never "you'll know who to call".
4. For agents and inspectors, "help each other's customers" is in there somewhere, in natural words (Jesse, 14 and 15 Oct). The property-manager opening doesn't use it.
5. One ask: 10 minutes, this week or next.
6. Jesse's opt-out line, word for word: "If you don't see us working together, no worries at all. Just reply and let me know and I won't email again."
7. A blank line, then "Cheers," / "Jesse" / logo signature / "Smart Contracting Ltd | PH 03 567 0876".
8. Never a second cold email to someone already contacted, and never anyone on the suppression list.

## 3. Partner documents: how they should sound

For one-pagers, campaign emails, after-call emails, the agreement and the FAQ.

- **Short and plain.** Short sentences, everyday words. If a line can go, it goes.
- **Kiwi, not salesy.** Write it the way Jesse would say it to a business owner down the road. "Cheers", "no worries", "a quick chat" are fine. No American sales talk: no "reach out", "circle back", "touch base", "game-changer", "unlock", "special offer just for you", "don't miss out", "limited time".
- **No hype.** No exclamation marks to sell, no capitals for emphasis, no "act now". Mike's point (Oct 13): pushing doesn't come naturally to Kiwis. So don't push. Be useful and ask plainly.
- **NZ spelling.** Colour, organise, neighbour, metre, centre, licence (noun), programme (a scheme or a TV show; "program" only for computer software). "Spouting" for gutters is fine in Canterbury.
- **Nothing made up.** No invented results, numbers, testimonials, reviews, prices, guarantees or turnaround times. If a document needs a fact we don't have, leave a visible gap: `[… to confirm with Jesse]`.
- **Honest about the problem.** Older Christchurch homes often still have earthenware drains, and many were cracked in the quakes; you can't see it because it's underground. Say "often" and "can", never "your drains are broken". In customer-facing copy, don't state the reader's insurance position.
- **Help first.** Every piece should be useful to the reader even if they never call.
- **No mention of AI or software** in anything a partner or customer reads. It comes from Jesse and Smart Contracting.
- **Sign-off** on Jesse's emails: "Cheers," / "Jesse" / "Smart Contracting Ltd | PH 03 567 0876". A partner's campaign is signed by the partner.

## Radar replies

For replies **Jesse posts himself, from his own personal Facebook profile**, to a homeowner's public post asking for drain help: blocked drains, tree roots, old earthenware, flooding sections, quake damage, pre-purchase checks. The radar only drafts; it never posts.

**Rules**
- **Help first.** One useful, practical tip, or the likely cause, honestly hedged ("often", "could be", "worth checking").
- **Then a light offer.** A CCTV check shows exactly what's going on, and it comes with a written report.
- **Sign off** as Jesse from Smart Contracting, 03 567 0876.
- **2 to 4 sentences.**
- **No** links, prices, hard sell, fee talk, or claims we can't back.
- **Don't reply at all** if they already have a tradie booked, or if they're just venting about a council issue.

**Example 1** (slow drain that gurgles, older house):
> Gurgling and a slow drain in an older place is often tree roots or a cracked joint in the old earthenware pipe, though it's worth checking the gully trap grate is clear first. If it keeps coming back, a CCTV check will show exactly what's going on down there, and you get a written report either way. Jesse from Smart Contracting, 03 567 0876.

**Example 2** (buying an older home, wants the drains checked):
> Good on you for thinking about the drains before you buy. They're underground, so they're easy to miss in a normal look around, and a lot of older Christchurch homes still have earthenware pipes. A CCTV check shows what condition they're actually in, and you get a written report to help with your decision. Jesse from Smart Contracting, 03 567 0876.
