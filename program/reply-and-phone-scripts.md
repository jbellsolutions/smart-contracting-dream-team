# Reply and phone scripts

**Note first:** on 12 Oct Jesse said he had "refined the docs a bit to match how we're pricing and working". His versions of the Oct 9 playbook, routine and one-pager haven't been shared yet. **When he shares them, his versions replace these.** Until then, this is the Oct 9 reply and phone playbook draft, tidied up, with Jesse's own phrases kept where it had them.

These are a guide, not a script to read out. Use your own words.

## The three rules

1. **Get it off email.** Thank them, answer in a line, ask for their number and a good time to call.
2. **No answer in an hour or two? Text them.** Short and friendly.
3. **Every partner call ends with a date.** Even a rough one, for their first campaign.

Say it the way you'd say it to a mate who runs a business down the road. You're not asking for favours: you're offering their clients something useful, and paying the partner for it.

**Before you dial,** take a breath. "I just want to help, I just want to connect with this business owner and help as many of his customers as possible" (the Oct 7 call).

## 3. They ask for a price

Never quote on email. Every drain is different: CCTV first, then a written report and a quote.

**By email:**
> Hi {first name},
>
> Thanks for asking. Every property is different, so we price to the job and what's best for the client. A two-bedroom with one drain line is a different job to a big older house with three bathrooms. We start with a CCTV check, and you get a written report and a clear quote if anything needs doing.
>
> Can I give you a quick call and run through a couple of typical jobs? What's the best number and a good time?
>
> Cheers,
> Jesse
> Smart Contracting Ltd | PH 03 567 0876

**On the phone:**
"Fair question. Everything we do is fitted to the property and what's best for the client. A quick check on a two-bedroom isn't the same as a full report and repair quote on a big older house. What do your clients usually need?" Then listen.

If you haven't settled scope or price: "Let me check with Mike, who prices our work, and come back to you by {day}." That's a fine answer.

If they compare you with someone cheaper, ask what that price actually included before you say anything about yours.

## 4. The campaign, in plain words (the "list swap")

Say what you'd like to do. Don't claim a track record we don't have yet.

"Here's what I had in mind. Most referral setups are 'send us someone when it comes up', and then nothing much happens. I'd like to do it a bit differently. You send a short email or text to your clients, say your sellers or past clients, letting them know it's worth getting their drains checked. We write it for you, you check it, and you send it from your own system, so your list never leaves you. We look after anyone who gets in touch, and you get 5% of the job value, paid once the customer's invoice is settled in full. It's five minutes of your time, and it means you're the one who gave your clients a heads-up about something they didn't know they had. Probably not something anyone's offered you before."

- For inspectors, swap "your sellers or past clients" for "the people you inspect for".
- For property managers, swap it for "your owners and landlords".
- *Only if Jesse has already said yes for this partner:* "And we'd be happy to tell our customers about you too." Never offer this by default.

## 5. Discovery questions (ask, then listen)

- "Roughly how many listings (or inspections, or properties) do you look after a month?"
- "When drains come up, what usually happens?"
- "Who do you use for drainage at the moment? How's that going?"
- "Do you keep in touch with past clients, by email, text or a newsletter? Roughly how many people?"
- "How many of your customers do you think know they've probably got old drains that need looking at?"

The list question matters most: it tells you how big the list is and how the campaign would go out.

## 6. The close

> **"Cool, let's do a campaign. How many people are on your list?"**

Then pin the date: "How about I send you a draft email by {day}, you check it, and it goes out to your {sellers / past clients / owners} on {date}? Does that work?"

Before you hang up, be clear on three things:
- **What** they'll send: an email or text, which we write and they approve.
- **To whom:** which list, and roughly how many people.
- **When:** a date, or at least a week. No firm date? "Even a rough week is fine. Shall we say the week of {date}, and I'll check in before then?"

Rather meet? Christchurch is small: "Can I buy you a coffee and show you how it works?" Book the day and place.

Keep it to what the crews can handle: "I'd rather start with one campaign and do it properly."

## 8. Objections

**"I'm busy" / "not now":** "No problem. When's better? I'll call you then and keep it short." Book the time, or note the month they give and follow up then.
By email:
> Hi {first name},
>
> No problem, thanks for letting me know. When would be a better time to talk? If you send me a number, I'll give you a quick call then and keep it short.
>
> Cheers,
> Jesse
> Smart Contracting Ltd | PH 03 567 0876

**"We already have a plumber / drainage company":** "That's fine, I'm not asking you to drop anyone. This is a heads-up to your clients about getting their drains checked. It's extra work for both of us, not instead of anyone. Want to try one and see how it goes?"
For property managers: "I'm not looking to replace your contractors" (Jesse's own line, Oct 20).

**"What about my list? I'm not handing it over":** "You don't. We never see your list. You send the email from your own system, to people you're already allowed to email, and the only people we hear from are the ones who choose to get in touch."

**"What if the job goes wrong?":** "The job's between the customer and us, not you. If anything isn't right, they come to us and we deal with it." [what we say about workmanship or a guarantee: to confirm with Jesse]. Don't promise a guarantee until Jesse sets one.

**"I need to ask my principal / business partner":** "Makes sense. Would it help if I came in and explained it to you both? Ten minutes, or I'll bring the coffees. When are they around?" Leave with a date for that chat.

**"We can't promote one company":** "Understood. A lot of offices keep a short list of trades they trust, and we'd be happy to be one name on it. Or the email can be about getting drains checked, with us as one option."

**"We're covered" / "not looking for suppliers":** don't argue. "No worries at all, good to hear you're covered." If it fits, one try with the angle Jesse used on Oct 20: "I've got an idea I'd like to run past you: helping your owners find out what condition their drains are in." If they say no again, log a final decline and never contact them again.

## 10. After the call

Log it straight away (two minutes):
- Tell the Revenue Partner in Slack how it went, in a line: "Called {name}, {business}: keen, campaign to {list} in {month}" or "not now, try {month}". It updates the ledger.
- A short note on the contact in LeadConnector, so Mike can see it too.

Then:
- **Same day:** send the follow-up text, so the date is in writing:
  > Thanks for the chat {first name}. As agreed: I'll send the draft email by {day}, and it goes to your {list} on {date}. Any questions, give me a call. Jesse, Smart Contracting
- **If they're in:** the Revenue Partner adds them as a partner, drafts the welcome email (`emails.md`) for your yes, and drafts their campaign from `campaigns/`.
- **Before the campaign date:** you check the campaign email, then it goes to the partner to approve. They send it from their own system.
- **Campaign day:** check it went out. Call anyone who gets in touch the same day.
- **A few days later:** a quick call or text to the partner: "Thanks for sending that. Here's who's been in touch so far."
- **Every job that comes through:** logged against their reference code. Once the customer's invoice is settled in full, the fee goes into the next monthly payout.
- **A few months later:** go back and ask for the next campaign.
