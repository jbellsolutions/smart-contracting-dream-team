# Partner types, in order

Who to go after, why their customers need a drain check, the moment that matters, what to offer them, and the opening line Jesse has set for them. Anything marked *(inferred)* is our reasoning, not something Jesse or a call said. Revise the order once real replies and jobs come in.

**Never in a cold email:** the fee, money, "pay" or "commission" (see `voice.md`). The "what to offer them" lines are for the call and for signed partners.

---

## First tier (Jesse's picks)

Jesse chose building inspectors and real-estate agents on the Oct 7 call; he added property managers with their own opening on Oct 20.

### 1. Building inspectors

- **Why their customers need a drain check:** a buyer is about to spend a lot on an older home. Drains run underground, so a look around the house says little about them *(inferred)*. Earthenware laterals cracked in the quakes are exactly the kind of problem nobody sees.
- **The moment that matters:** the pre-purchase (and pre-sale) inspection, when the inspector notes something about drainage or can't check it.
- **What to offer them:** a local drainage team to hand their clients when drains come up, a CCTV check with a written report every time (it sits well next to their own report), and 5% of the job value, paid once the customer's invoice is settled in full, on any job that comes from their clients. Talk about the fee on the call, not by email.
- **Watch for:** inspectors may care about looking independent when they recommend a contractor *(inferred)*. Let them decide how they word it to their clients; the campaign email they approve is theirs.
- **Opening line (Jesse's agent-and-inspector structure, Oct 9; kept as is on Oct 20):**
  > Jesse from Smart Contracting here. We're a local Christchurch drainage team.
  >
  > [one verified detail about them, rounded off so it connects to us]
  >
  > We do CCTV drain inspections and give owners a written report, whether it's a general check before a sale or a specific concern.

### 2. Real-estate agents

- **Why their customers need a drain check:** plenty of Christchurch homes still have old earthenware drains. A drain problem found late, by the buyer's inspector, can stall a sale or knock the price down (Jesse's one-pager draft).
- **The moment that matters:** before listing. A pre-sale drain check with a CCTV report lets the seller list with confidence: either the drains are fine, or they're fixed on the seller's terms before buyers see them. Also past clients who bought older homes.
- **What to offer them:** a fast, reliable local name to hand their sellers, a written report the seller can use in the sale, a campaign email to their sellers or past clients written for them, and 5% of the job value, paid once the customer's invoice is settled in full (call only).
- **Watch for:** agents may have to tell their clients about any referral fee they receive. Check with the agent before agreeing a fee, so nobody is caught out (see `compliance-nz.md`). Some offices "can't promote one company": offer to be one name on their trusted list, or make the email about getting drains checked before selling.
- **Opening line:** the same agent-and-inspector structure as above (Oct 9). Example verified detail from Jesse's own draft: "I noticed [agency] is based in [suburb], so I thought I'd introduce us as a nearby drainage contact for your clients."

### 3. Property managers

- **Why their customers need a drain check:** landlords and owners are responsible for the drains on their rentals. Rentals wear drains quietly, and a blocked or broken drain turns into an urgent call-out and an unhappy tenant *(inferred)*.
- **The moment that matters:** a change of tenants, a routine inspection, an owner planning maintenance or about to sell, and after heavy rain.
- **What to offer them:** a way to help their owners find out what condition their drains are in, a written report for the owner's records, a campaign email to their owners and landlords, and 5% of the job value, paid once the customer's invoice is settled in full (call only).
- **Watch for:** property managers already have contractors. Two replied "we're covered / not looking for suppliers" to the contractor-style opening (Oct 20). Don't pitch as a replacement contractor.
- **Opening line (Jesse, Oct 20, for property managers only):**
  > Jesse from Smart Contracting here. We're a local Christchurch drainage team. I'm not looking to replace your contractors. I'm looking at how we could help your owners and landlords find out what condition their drains are in, with a CCTV check and a written report.
  >
  > Do you have 10 minutes this week or next? If you don't see us working together, no worries at all. Just reply and let me know and I won't email again.

  Keep one verified detail about them or their agency in the draft, as before.

## Second tier: trades who are already at the house

Justin's advice (Sep 16 and Oct 7 calls): other contractors are "the low-hanging fruit, the best ones", because they're already going to the house. Jesse's first picks come first; trades come next.

### 4. Electricians, plumbers, builders and roofers

- **Why their customers need a drain check:** they work in older homes every day. A renovation, new bathroom or kitchen, new downpipes or a re-roof all touch or sit near the drains and stormwater *(inferred)*.
- **The moment that matters:** a renovation or bathroom/kitchen job, roof or spouting work that changes where stormwater goes, or the trade noticing slow drains, damp ground or smells on site *(inferred)*.
- **What to offer them:** a drainage team they can hand the job to when it isn't theirs, a campaign email to their past customers written for them, and 5% of the job value, paid once the customer's invoice is settled in full (call only). For a trade whose work genuinely suits our customers (for example a plumber who does the kind of plumbing we don't), Jesse may also agree to tell our customers about them. That's selective, on Jesse's yes only, never part of the standard offer.
- **Watch for:** plumbers can overlap with drainage work *(inferred)*. Keep "referral" (they send a customer to us) apart from "subcontract" (they hire us for part of their job): only referrals earn the fee.
- **Opening line:** none set by Jesse yet. *(inferred)* Use the same structure as agents and inspectors, with a line about helping each other's customers instead of a line about sales or inspections, and show Jesse the first draft before any send.

Also named on the Sep 16 call: HVAC installers and landscapers. Treat them like the trades above.
